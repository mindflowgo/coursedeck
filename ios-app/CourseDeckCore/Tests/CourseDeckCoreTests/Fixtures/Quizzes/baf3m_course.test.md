## Answers & Detailed Solutions

01) C - The fundamental accounting equation is Assets − Liabilities = Owner's Equity. This equation shows that the owner's claim on the business assets is what remains after all creditor claims are paid.

02) C - A corporation is a separate legal entity from its owners. It can sue and be sued, and shareholders have limited liability, meaning they risk only their investment.

03) C - Owner's equity = Assets − Liabilities = $85,000 − $32,000 = $53,000. This represents Maria's claim on the business assets after all debts are paid.

04) B - Accounting standards such as IFRS and ASPE provide a common set of rules so that financial statements are comparable, reliable, and transparent for external users such as investors, creditors, and government agencies.

05) A - A creditor is primarily interested in the company's ability to pay its debts when due. The balance sheet shows assets, liabilities, and owner's equity, which help assess creditworthiness.

06) B - In a corporation, shareholders have limited liability. If the corporation goes bankrupt, shareholders lose only their investment in the company, not their personal assets.

07) B - Assets are typically ordered by liquidity, from most liquid (e.g., cash) to least liquid (e.g., equipment, building). This helps users assess the company's ability to convert assets to cash.

08) B - The primary purpose of a trial balance is to verify that total debit balances equal total credit balances in the general ledger, confirming mathematical accuracy.

09) B - Owner's equity = Assets − Liabilities = $250,000 − $90,000 = $160,000.

10) A - The firm received office supplies (asset increases, so debit) and owes cash later (liability increases, so credit). The correct entry is Debit Office Supplies $400; Credit Accounts Payable $400.

11) A - The double-entry system means every transaction affects at least two accounts, and total debits must always equal total credits. This keeps the accounting equation in balance.

12) A - Net income = Revenue − Expenses = $50,000 − $35,000 = $15,000. Since revenue exceeds expenses, it is a net income.

13) B - A merchandising business buys goods from manufacturers, wholesalers, or importers and resells them to customers at a profit. It differs from a service business and a manufacturing business.

14) A - COGS = Beginning Inventory + Purchases − Purchase Returns − Purchase Discounts + Transportation-in − Ending Inventory = $20,000 + $60,000 − $2,000 − $1,000 + $3,000 − $15,000 = $65,000.

15) C - HST remitted = HST Payable − HST Recoverable = $130 − $78 = $52. The business acts as a collection agent and only remits the net tax.

16) A - Perpetual inventory keeps a continuous record of inventory and cost of goods sold; periodic inventory determines inventory by physical count at the end of the period.

17) B - HST Payable is a liability account. It records the HST collected from customers on sales, which the business owes to the Canada Revenue Agency.

18) A - Bad Debts Expense = 2% × $200,000 = $4,000. The adjusting entry is Debit Bad Debts Expense $4,000; Credit Allowance for Doubtful Accounts $4,000.

19) B - The main purpose of internal control is to safeguard assets, ensure reliable accounting records, prevent theft and fraud, and reduce errors.

20) B - The fund must be replenished for the amount spent, which is the total of the vouchers: $140. This brings the fund back to its established $150 balance.

21) B - An NSF cheque is a customer payment that was returned due to insufficient funds. It is deducted from the book balance because the company must reverse the original deposit.

22) B - A current ratio of 3.5:1 means the company has $3.50 in current assets for every $1.00 in current liabilities. This indicates strong short-term liquidity.

23) B - Current ratio = Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities = $350,000 ÷ $290,000 = 1.2069, which rounds to 1.21:1.

24) B - Debt-to-equity ratio = Total Liabilities ÷ Total Equity = $260,000 ÷ $210,000 = 1.238, which rounds to 1.24. This means creditors have $1.24 for every $1.00 of owner's equity.

## Comprehensive Short Answer / Case Problems – Answers

01) Maria’s Design Studio
a) **Recommended form: Corporation.** A corporation is a separate legal entity and provides limited liability, so Maria’s personal assets are protected if the business fails. A sole proprietorship or general partnership would expose her to unlimited personal liability.
b) **Owner’s equity** = Assets − Liabilities = ($20,000 Cash + $5,000 Equipment) − $8,000 Bank Loan = $25,000 − $8,000 = **$17,000**.
c) **Opening journal entry:**
   - Debit Cash $20,000
   - Debit Equipment $5,000
   - Credit Bank Loan $8,000
   - Credit Maria, Capital $17,000
d) **Balance Sheet (Report Form):**
   **Assets**
   - Cash $20,000
   - Equipment $5,000
   - Total Assets $25,000
   **Liabilities**
   - Bank Loan $8,000
   **Owner’s Equity**
   - Maria, Capital $17,000
   - Total Liabilities and Owner’s Equity $25,000

02) LR Law Firm
a) **Income Statement** for year ended Dec 31, 2024:
   - Fees Income $50,000
   - Expenses:
     - Salaries Expense $18,000
     - Rent Expense $5,000
     - Utilities Expense $2,000
     - Supplies Expense $1,500
     - Depreciation Expense $3,000
     - Total Expenses $29,500
   - **Net Income $20,500**
b) **Statement of Owner’s Equity**:
   - L. Roberts, Capital, Jan 1 $35,000
   - Add: Net Income $20,500
   - Less: Drawings $10,000
   - **L. Roberts, Capital, Dec 31 $45,500**
c) **Balance Sheet** at Dec 31, 2024:
   **Assets**
   - Current Assets:
     - Cash $29,750
     - Accounts Receivable $4,600
     - Office Supplies $900
     - Total Current Assets $35,250
   - Fixed Assets:
     - Equipment $30,000
     - Less: Accumulated Depreciation $3,000
     - Net Equipment $27,000
   - **Total Assets $62,250**
   **Liabilities**
   - Current Liabilities:
     - Accounts Payable $2,400
   - Long-term Liabilities:
     - Bank Loan $14,350
   - Total Liabilities $16,750
   **Owner’s Equity**
   - L. Roberts, Capital $45,500
   - **Total Liabilities and Owner’s Equity $62,250**
d) **Closing entries:**
   1. Debit Fees Income $50,000; Credit Income Summary $50,000.
   2. Debit Income Summary $29,500; Credit Salaries Expense $18,000; Credit Rent Expense $5,000; Credit Utilities Expense $2,000; Credit Supplies Expense $1,500; Credit Depreciation Expense $3,000.
   3. Debit Income Summary $20,500; Credit L. Roberts, Capital $20,500.
   4. Debit L. Roberts, Capital $10,000; Credit L. Roberts, Drawings $10,000.
e) **Current ratio** = Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities = $35,250 ÷ $2,400 = **14.69:1**. This is extremely high, indicating very strong liquidity. The firm has ample current assets to cover short-term debts, though it may be holding too much idle cash or receivables.

03) Fresh Mart
a) **Cost of Goods Sold** = Beginning Inventory $20,000 + Purchases $60,000 − Purchase Returns $2,000 − Purchase Discounts $1,000 + Transportation-in $3,000 − Ending Inventory $15,000 = **$65,000**.
b) **Journal entries:**
   - Purchase on credit:
     - Debit Purchases $10,000
     - Debit HST Recoverable $1,300
     - Credit Accounts Payable $11,300
   - Sale on account:
     - Debit Accounts Receivable $22,600
     - Credit Sales $20,000
     - Credit HST Payable $2,600
c) **Classified Income Statement** for March:
   - Sales $90,000
   - Less: Cost of Goods Sold:
     - Beginning Inventory $20,000
     - Purchases $60,000
     - Less: Purchase Returns $2,000
     - Less: Purchase Discounts $1,000
     - Add: Transportation-in $3,000
     - Goods Available for Sale $80,000
     - Less: Ending Inventory $15,000
     - Cost of Goods Sold $65,000
   - **Gross Profit $25,000**
   - Operating Expenses $25,000
   - **Net Income $0**
d) **Gross profit margin** = Gross Profit ÷ Net Sales = $25,000 ÷ $90,000 = **27.78%**. This is a moderate margin. However, because operating expenses equal gross profit, the business is breaking even. Management should focus on reducing operating expenses or increasing sales margins to generate net income.

04) R. Johnson Co.
a) **Internal control weaknesses and improvements:**
   - Weakness 1: One employee handles cash, records sales, and prepares deposits — a lack of separation of duties. Improvement: Separate custody, recording, and reconciliation among different employees.
   - Weakness 2: No daily deposit or independent reconciliation. Improvement: Deposit all cash intact daily and have a different employee prepare the bank reconciliation.
b) **Bank Reconciliation:**
   - Bank Statement Balance $5,000
   - Add: Deposit in transit $600
   - Less: Outstanding cheques $800
   - **Adjusted Bank Balance $4,800**
   - Book Balance $5,020
   - Less: NSF cheque $200
   - Less: Bank service charge $50
   - Add: Interest earned $30
   - **Adjusted Book Balance $4,800**
c) **Adjusting entries:**
   1. Debit Accounts Receivable $200; Credit Cash $200 (NSF cheque).
   2. Debit Bank Charges Expense $50; Credit Cash $50 (service charge).
   3. Debit Cash $30; Credit Interest Income $30 (interest earned).
d) **Ethical principle violated:** The **Objectivity Principle** and **Full Disclosure Principle**. Omitting the NSF cheque would misstate cash and intentionally deceive the bank, violating professional integrity and ethical accounting practices.
e) **Current ratio** = Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities = $50,000 ÷ $25,000 = **2:1**. This indicates good liquidity and is likely acceptable to a lender.
