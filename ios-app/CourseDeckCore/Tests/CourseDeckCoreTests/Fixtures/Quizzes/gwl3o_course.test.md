## Answers & Detailed Solutions

01) C — Essential skills are the foundational skills that go beyond basic literacy and numeracy. Unit 1.4 lists nine Essential Skills: reading, numeracy, writing, digital, problem solving, communication, collaboration, adaptability, and creativity/innovation. They are used in nearly every occupation and daily life, but at different complexity levels.

02) B — Unit 2.1 defines emotional intelligence as the ability to recognize, understand, manage, and use emotions effectively in oneself and others. It is a learnable workplace asset, not a fixed IQ trait.

03) B — Unit 3.2 explains that apprenticeship combines approximately 80% paid on-the-job training with 20% classroom instruction in a skilled trade. It is government-regulated and leads to a Certificate of Qualification.

04) C — Unit 1.2 teaches that self-assessments such as Holland’s RIASEC are not definitive verdicts. They are invitations to explore. A student should reflect on whether results ring true and consider how multiple interests, values, and types can combine.

05) B — The Creating Pathways to Success model is cyclical. After an experiential learning experience, students reflect on what they learned, revise self-knowledge and goals, and use that insight to explore new opportunities. This is not a reason to stop planning.

06) B — Unit 4.2 emphasizes that the hidden job market is accessed through networking, informational interviews, referrals, social media, and word of mouth. Many desirable jobs are never publicly advertised.

07) B — Unit 1.5 distinguishes a PSA from a résumé. A résumé lists experiences; a PSA is a persuasive, evidence-based argument that shows why those experiences make the applicant a strong fit for a specific opportunity.

08) B — Unit 3.2 defines a scholarship as an award often based on academic or other achievement, while a bursary is usually based on financial need. The classmate’s statement confuses the two terms.

09) B — Unit 3.4 explains that economic trends affect markets, industries, and employment, while societal trends affect how people live, think, and behave. Together they create employment-related trends that influence the future of work.

10) C — Unit 2.1 identifies interpersonal intelligence as the ability to understand and interact effectively with others. Priya reads the customer’s emotion, adjusts her tone, and responds helpfully, which clearly demonstrates interpersonal intelligence.

11) A — Unit 3.2 describes apprenticeship as a pathway that combines paid on-the-job training with classroom instruction in a skilled trade. It suits Devon’s desire to earn soon, learn hands-on, avoid a four-year degree, and enter a growing trade.

12) B — Unit 4.5 and 4.6 stress person–job fit. A strong application package is targeted: the résumé and cover letter should mirror the job ad’s language and requirements and provide specific evidence that the applicant matches the employer’s needs.

### Comprehensive Short Answer / Case Problems — Suggested Answers

01)  
a) **Creating Pathways to Success four-step inquiry process:**  
- **Who am I?** Maya should reflect on her Holland Social/Investigative results, values, interests, and strengths.  
- **What are my opportunities?** She should research nursing, medical laboratory technology, and paramedic programs, job duties, and labour market demand.  
- **Who do I want to become?** She should compare which roles best fit her helping and problem-solving preferences and set a tentative goal.  
- **What is my plan for achieving my goals?** She should map required courses, volunteer/co-op experiences, and post-secondary applications.

b) Maya should treat the Holland assessment as an invitation to explore, not a label. She should ask whether the Social and Investigative descriptions ring true, remember that most people are combinations of types, and use the results to generate options rather than limit them.

c) Essential Skills could include communication, problem solving, digital, or collaboration. Employability skills could include teamwork, reliability, or adaptability. To improve a gap, she could join a volunteer role, practise active listening, or complete a first-aid course.

d) A PSA would be a persuasive, autobiographical argument showing why Maya is a strong fit for the college health program. It would use specific experiences as evidence of caring, problem solving, and commitment. A résumé would mainly list her education, skills, and experience. The PSA must connect every experience forward to the target program.

02)  
a) Six students can be appropriate for a one-day food-bank project if the activity has enough tasks and supervision. Group size should relate directly to the activity: smaller groups for focused tasks, larger groups for sorting, packing, or event support.

b) The controlling member is demonstrating **fight**—aggressive, “I’m right, you’re wrong,” likely lose-lose. The silent member is demonstrating **flight**—passive, avoiding conflict, likely win-lose. The team should aim for **flow**/assertive win-win. First two conflict-resolution steps: identify/define the problem respectfully, and discuss it while listening to all perspectives. Then make a plan, implement it, and evaluate.

c) Useful intelligences could be interpersonal intelligence for team communication and logical-mathematical intelligence for organizing donations. Positive team roles could include Encourager, Recorder, Summarizer/clarifier, or Evaluator. Each helps the team stay focused, included, and organized.

d) The volunteer experience could become a portfolio artifact if it is purposeful, organized, reflective, and connected to career goals. It can show employability skills such as teamwork, communication, reliability, and community involvement.

03)  
a) Jordan should use career clusters to see that electrician fits Architecture & Construction or a skilled-trades cluster, while graphic designer fits Arts, A/V Technology & Communications. He should check LMI from Job Bank for wages, outlook, education requirements, and regional demand.

b) Electrician: apprenticeship, paid on-the-job training plus classroom instruction, Certificate of Qualification, earn while learning. Graphic designer: college/university pathway, tuition costs, diploma/degree, portfolio-based work. Financing may include scholarships, bursaries, savings, OSAP, or paid work.

c) Trends could include computerization/technology, automation, aging population, contract/gig work, and lifelong learning. Jordan should expect ongoing training and certification. Lifelong learning means using formal and informal learning throughout life to adapt to workplace changes.

d) If refused because of disability, the Ontario Human Rights Code protects against discrimination based on disability and requires accommodation unless there is a bona fide occupational requirement. If unpaid overtime violates the Employment Standards Act, he should document the issue, discuss it with the employer/HR, and if unresolved, contact the Ministry of Labour.

04)  
a) Amara should use multiple work-search strategies: online ads, public employment centres, networking, informational interviews, cold calls, and referrals. She should build a contacts network and track applications on a job-tracking sheet with date, position, company, source, contact info, and follow-up notes.

b) She should tailor her résumé and cover letter to the job ad by mirroring its language and matching required qualities with specific evidence. Her application package should include the job ad, targeted résumé, focused cover letter, prepared interview answers, follow-up plan, and a letter to self.

c) Interview strategies include using power words, preparing specific examples, researching the company, practising in a quiet space, sending a professional thank-you follow-up, and managing her digital footprint through social media audits and Googling her name.

d) Amara is experiencing imposed change. She should recognize that resistance often comes from fear of the unknown and expectation of loss. Using transition phases such as denial, resistance, exploration, and commitment, she can acknowledge feelings, assess options, take action, and evaluate progress. In a letter to self, she might define what success means and write from the future about how she adapted positively.
