Here is a completed version of the table. Examples can be changed to match your own community.

| Type of business ownership | Example from your community | Advantage of this type of business ownership | Disadvantage of this type of business ownership | How to overcome the disadvantage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sole proprietorship** | A local plumber, tutor, independent bakery owner, or small grocery store | Owner keeps all profits; easy and inexpensive to start; possible tax savings because business income is taxed as personal income | **Unlimited liability** — owner’s personal assets are at risk; limited capital; lack of continuity | Buy **business liability insurance**; reinvest profits; apply for small business loans; eventually **incorporate** to gain **limited liability** |
| **Partnership** | A law firm, accounting practice, real estate firm, or doctor’s group | **Shared workload, talents, start-up costs, and investment capital**; partners can complement each other’s skills | **Unlimited personal liability** for general partners; need for compatibility; divided authority; lack of continuity | Form a **limited partnership** so limited partners risk only their investment; create a **partnership agreement** that clearly defines roles and decision-making |
| **Corporation** | **Manulife Financial** (public corporation) or **Giant Tiger** (private corporation) | **Limited liability** for shareholders; transferable ownership; more access to capital and talent; possible tax advantage | Expensive to organize; more legal requirements and government regulation; **double taxation** of net income | Keep accurate accounting records; use **tax planning** such as salary vs. dividends; start as a **private corporation** to keep ownership control; budget for legal and regulatory costs |

**Key terms to remember:**  
- **Unlimited liability** means the owner’s personal assets are at risk for business debts.  
- **Limited liability** means shareholders risk only the amount they invested in the corporation.  
- A **general partnership** exposes all partners to unlimited liability.  
- A **limited partnership** protects limited partners beyond their registered investment.  
- A **corporation** is a **separate legal entity** that pays its own taxes and has an unlimited life.
