# 1.1 Introduction to Accounting Pop Quiz

## Questions

01) [Knowledge] Which term describes the portion of a business's assets that the owner can truly claim once every debt to creditors has been settled?
<Multiple-Choice>
- [ ] Total liabilities
- [ ] Owner's equity
- [ ] Liquid assets
- [ ] Net income

02) [Thinking] Clara has $58,000 in assets and $16,500 in liabilities, and her bank is reviewing her $20,000 loan application. Which statement best explains why the bank focuses on her equity rather than simply on her total assets?
<Multiple-Choice>
- [ ] Equity tells the bank exactly how much cash Clara has in her bank account right now.
- [ ] Total assets alone determine whether Clara can repay the loan, so equity is irrelevant.
- [ ] Equity is simply her total assets minus the value of her car, which the bank cannot seize.
- [ ] Equity shows what Clara actually owns after creditors' claims are removed, revealing whether she has a real financial cushion to repay new debt.

--

03) [Application] A student lists the following: cash $2,500, a laptop worth $1,200, a bicycle worth $400, a student loan of $6,000, and credit card debt of $800. What is her financial position, and what does it mean for a loan application?
<Multiple-Choice>
- [ ] Equity of $2,700 — she is in a healthy financial position and should easily qualify.
- [ ] Equity of $6,800 — she owns more than she owes, so the bank will approve the loan.
- [ ] Negative equity of $2,700 — her liabilities exceed her assets, so a lender would likely refuse the loan.
- [ ] Equity of $4,100 — her total assets, which guarantees loan approval.

04) [Communication] Which statement most accurately communicates the difference between accounting and bookkeeping?
<Multiple-Choice>
- [ ] Accounting is the overall recording, controlling, analyzing, and interpreting of financial information, whereas bookkeeping is only the initial recording stage.
- [ ] Bookkeeping involves analyzing and interpreting financial statements, while accounting is limited to the manual recording of transactions.
- [ ] The two terms are interchangeable because both describe the complete cycle of recording and interpreting financial data.
- [ ] Accounting is performed only by external auditors, while bookkeeping is performed only by business owners.

05) [Fill-in-the-Blank] Since 2011, Canadian GAAP has been replaced by IFRS for publicly traded companies and by _______ for private businesses that do not have stock traded on a public exchange.

--

06) [Fill-in-the-Blank] An _______ is an outside professional who verifies that a company's accounts have been compiled in a way that complies with International Financial Reporting Standards.

--

## Answers
01) B - (explanation) Owner's equity (also called net worth or capital) is the difference between total assets and total liabilities; it represents the owner's residual claim on assets after creditors are paid first.
02) D - (explanation) Assets alone can be misleading because they may be financed by debt. Equity (assets − liabilities) reveals the owner's true stake and whether there is enough of a cushion to absorb a new loan, which is why banks compare the two figures.
03) C - (explanation) Assets = $2,500 + $1,200 + $400 = $4,100; Liabilities = $6,000 + $800 = $6,800; Equity = $4,100 − $6,800 = −$2,700. Because liabilities exceed assets, the lesson notes a financial institution would probably not grant a loan.
04) A - (explanation) Accounting covers the full cycle — recording, controlling, analyzing, and interpreting — while bookkeeping is only the first step: recording transactions in journals and ledgers.
05) ASPE (Accounting Standards for Private Enterprises) - (explanation) IFRS applies to publicly listed companies, while ASPE, set by the AcSB, applies to private enterprises without publicly traded stock.
06) Auditor - (explanation) An auditor is an external person who verifies that the accounts comply with IFRS, providing assurance to users of the financial statements.
