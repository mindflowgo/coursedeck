# 2.3 The Trial Balance Pop Quiz

## Questions
01) [Knowledge] What is the primary purpose of preparing a trial balance?
<Multiple-Choice>
- [ ] To determine the net income or net loss earned during the period
- [ ] To report the financial position of the business to external users such as banks
- [ ] To verify that total debits equal total credits in the ledger before financial statements are prepared
- [ ] To record each business transaction in the order in which it occurred

--

02) [Thinking] Which of the following errors would cause the debit and credit column totals of a trial balance to be unequal?
<Multiple-Choice>
- [ ] A $650 debit to Cash was posted, but the matching credit to Bank Loan was never recorded
- [ ] A $400 purchase of supplies was entered as $40 in both Office Supplies and Cash
- [ ] A $300 cash payment was debited to Accounts Receivable instead of Accounts Payable
- [ ] An entire transaction was left out of the ledger completely

--

03) [Application] The Mexicana Restaurant ledger shows Accounts Payable $16,020, Mortgage Payable $74,020, and L. Feliciano, Capital $201,400. When these balances are entered into the trial balance, the total of the credit column will be:
<Multiple-Choice>
- [ ] $90,040
- [ ] $201,400
- [ ] $307,460
- [ ] $291,440

--

04) [Communication] A classmate says, "Our trial balance balances, so our ledger must be error-free." Which response best corrects this reasoning?
<Multiple-Choice>
- [ ] "A trial balance only covers one month, so errors from other months are automatically corrected."
- [ ] "A trial balance only proves debits equal credits; it cannot detect errors such as using the wrong account, entering an incorrect but equal amount on both sides, or omitting a transaction entirely."
- [ ] "A trial balance is prepared for outsiders, so internal errors are reported to the bank instead."
- [ ] "A trial balance always balances because the accounting equation does not apply to all businesses."

--

05) [Fill-in-the-Blank] When preparing a trial balance, the accounts are listed in the same order as they appear in the _______, with asset accounts first, then liabilities, and finally owner's equity.

--

06) [Fill-in-the-Blank] When the debit column total equals the credit column total, the trial balance confirms that the principle of _______ has been followed, because a debit amount was recorded for every credit amount.

--

## Answers
01) C - (explanation) A trial balance is an internal listing of all account balances used to check that total debits still equal total credits (i.e., that the ledger is mathematically accurate); it does not measure profit or report to outsiders.
02) A - (explanation) Only omitting one side of a transaction makes the column totals unequal. Errors B and C are recorded equally on both sides, and error D removes both the debit and the credit, so all three still leave the trial balance in balance.
03) D - (explanation) $16,020 + $74,020 + $201,400 = $291,440, which matches the $291,440 total of the debit column (Cash $7,540 + Supplies $13,200 + Equipment $40,700 + Building $200,000 + Land $30,000).
04) B - (explanation) A balanced trial balance only proves that debits equal credits. Errors of principle (wrong account), equal-but-incorrect amounts, transpositions entered on both sides, and completely omitted transactions all leave the trial balance in balance.
05) Ledger - (explanation) The trial balance mirrors the order of the ledger: assets first, then liabilities, then owner's equity, with each balance placed in the debit or credit column as appropriate.
06) Double-entry accounting - (explanation) Equal debit and credit totals demonstrate that every transaction was recorded with equal debits and credits, which is the foundation of double-entry accounting.
